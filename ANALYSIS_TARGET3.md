## 1 · What the imagery shows (S2 2020, 3.8 km)

* **No pale mass** — unlike T2, the whole window is brown-green: vegetated slopes (38%), gullies, brown soil. The only notable bright linear feature is **MEX 2** (NE edge).
* **Target pixel:** brown-green mix, B/R 0.536, S 0.46 — indistinguishable from background slopes.
* **Grey patch S/SSE (~300 m):** modest rock exposure on the Palmita slopes; spectrally *redder* (B/R 0.47) — Fe-oxide-rich regolith or weathered andesite, not clay/carbonate blue-shift.
* Interpretation: T3 is **structurally/geologically on the corridor but surficially unmarked** in RGB. Its value is (a) bedrock exposure (steepest terrain of the three) and (b) position between the two anomalous targets.

## 4 · On-site test (≈1.5–2 h) — recommended after T2

Waypoints in `maps/t3/field_waypoints.csv` (11 points). Priority:

1. **RIDGE (SSE, 808 m, ~2 km):** summit lithology — is this the Palmita high on andesite, granodiorite, or Rosario conglomerate? Map any exposure; note structural grain (foliation/vein trends) — this ridge is the corridor's high ground.
2. **STEEP waypoints (36–39° slopes):** first clear bedrock found → sample lithology + alteration (sericite? chlorite? quartz veins? N65–75°E vein trends per SGM).
3. **D-1…D-5 (arroyo transect through the point, 360–1000 m):** stream-sediment sample pair — this drainage may catch water/sediment from both the T2-side (E) slopes; assay Au, Ag, Cu, Fe, Pb, Zn, Mo.
4. **MEX 2 approach (NE, ~2 km):** look for cut-bank exposures along the road (fresh face = free outcrop mapping) and any old works visible from the highway.

Accuracy expectations as for T1/T2: 15–30 m total discrepancy expected; flag >50 m.

How the satellite data is gathered

A reproducible, legal pipeline — no tile-server scraping of Google or Bing. Official APIs plus fully open data, all fetchable by script.

1 · Google & Bing — official routes only

Google data is used via Google Earth Engine (Sentinel-2, Landsat, ASTER, Sentinel-1) and the Photorealistic 3D Tiles API; Bing via the Bing Maps Imagery API (Aerial tiles to z18). Direct tile scraping violates ToS, is CAPTCHA-gated, and yields no spectral bands. The sub-meter base layer on this site is Esri World Imagery (Maxar-class) tiles; the 10 m spectral layer is the open Sentinel-2 2020 cloudless composite (EOX, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0); terrain uses SRTM-derived 10 m DEM tiles (USGS, public domain).

2 · Spectral & terrain screening per coordinate

For each coordinate the pipeline computes: B/R blue-shift proxy (Fe-oxide depletion / clay-calcite indicator in RGB mode), HSV surface classes (vegetation / soil / pale-tone / bright crust / shadow), and from the 10 m DEM: slope, aspect, hillshade and drainage transects. Full-band mode (free Earthdata login) adds NDVI, ferric-iron, clay and carbonate indices at 30 m.

3 · Geology cross-reference

Every site is cross-referenced against SGM Carta Geológico-Minera El Aguajito H11-B85, 1:50,000 (2009): Alisitos-terrane andesite/breccia (Kapa A-BvA), Cretaceous granodiorite–diorite intrusions, Late Cretaceous Rosario Fm, the La Turquesa Cu–Fe zona mineralizada, 14 documented workings, and the documented structure (NE–SW normals, N65–75°E veins, N–S relays, tabular brechas).

Frequently asked questions

What is located at 30.048522, -115.236173?

The point sits on the NW slope of Cerro la Turquesa (740 m), San Quintín Municipio, Baja California, at ~617 m. Surveys identify a km-scale pale argillic (clay-altered) surface ~1 km west, a straight NE–SW lineament ~1 km east (road vs fault scarp — to be checked in the field), and a turquoise-mining toponym at the site. It is the highest-scored target (6.3/10) in the La Turquesa Cu–Fe epithermal corridor.

How are the satellite maps for a coordinate produced?

A reproducible pipeline fetches, per coordinate: Sentinel-2 2020 cloudless composite (10 m) from EOX, Esri World Imagery (0.37–1.03 m/px) for sub-meter detail, and SRTM 10 m DEM tiles for slope/aspect/hillshade. Spectral indices (B/R blue-shift, HSV surface classes) and terrain statistics are computed per site, then cross-referenced with the SGM El Aguajito 1:50,000 (2009) geologic-mineral map.

What is the La Turquesa corridor?

A documented low-sulfidation epithermal Cu–Fe (±Au) zona mineralizada mapped by SGM (1:50,000, 2009) in the Alisitos terrane, ~30.05–30.15° N, with 14 abandoned Cu–Fe workings (El Cardonal with documented turquoise; San Martín with chrysocolla, azurite and malachite; La Turquesa core; El Hormiguero; La Bonita; 5 Minas; El Sacrificio; Josefina; Santa Martha; Santa Lucía; La Víbora; El Moro), controlled by NE–SW normal faults and N65–75°E vein sets.

Is this investment or drilling advice?

No — it is an independent remote-sensing and geology desk study. Under Mexico's 2023 Ley Minera, new mining concessions are granted only by public tender, are prohibited in natural protected areas and water-stressed zones, and require prior consultation and social-impact studies. Water availability in this arid corridor is the main regulatory risk.

Is using Google or Bing imagery here legal?

Yes. Google/Bing data is accessed only through official channels (Google Earth Engine, Bing Maps Imagery API); the sub-meter base layer is Esri World Imagery; the analysis layers are fully open (Copernicus Sentinel-2, USGS SRTM). Tile-server scraping of Google or Bing is not used anywhere in the pipeline.

How do I run my own coordinate?

Members unlock the Coordinate Runner (Members section): enter lat/lon and the page places the point on sub-meter imagery, shows point elevation, computes distances and bearings to the 3 targets, 36 pins and 14 documented workings, and produces a shareable link. The 36-pin CSV is also downloadable for GPS devices.

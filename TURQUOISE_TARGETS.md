# Where to Prospect for Turquoise — La Turquesa Cu-Fe Corridor (Baja California, MX)

**Basis:** SGM Carta Geológico-Minera El Aguajito H11-B85 1:50,000 (2009) + our T1/T2/T3 remote-sensing surveys (Aug 2026).
**Map:** `maps/turquoise_prospect_map.jpg` (corridor overview: 14 documented workings, 4 prospect zones, 3 surveyed targets)

## What turquoise needs here (the recipe)

Turquoise = Cu + Al + Si in the **oxidized, near-surface, arid** environment:
1. A **Cu source** (the documented epithermal Cu-Fe system: chalcopyrite, bornite, pyrite in brecciated granodiorite + Alisitos andesite)
2. An **Al source** (argillic alteration — kaolinite/montmorillonite; SGM reports it "in most of the sheet")
3. **Oxidation + fresh exposure** (arroyo walls, fault scarps, old pit walls, brechas)

Indicator minerals to hunt: **blue-green spots, chrysocolla, malachite, azurite, jarosite**, white clay/silica staining. If you see malachite/azurite, turquoise is a prospect; if you see chrysocolla, you're in the right zone.

## RANKED prospect areas

### 🥇 ZONE A — El Cardonal / Esperanza / La Prieta complex (30.067, -115.376) — **BEST**
The only place SGM documents **actual turquoise**, plus the strongest structural package:
* **El Cardonal** — "mina principal de la región"; turquoise documented; controlled by **normal fault N82°E / 32° NW** (the fluid pathway)
* **La Prieta** — **open pit** with NE trend: fresh pit walls = free exposure of the old oxidized Cu zone. First stop, walk the walls with a hand lens.
* **Esperanza** — **veins N65°–75°E converging** at ranchería La Prieta (convergence = focused fluid flow = best mineralization)
* All three within ~1–2 km; access per SGM: Transpeninsular (MEX 2) → dirt roads N/S. ~10–13 km from your T3.

### 🥈 ZONE B — San Martín (30.146, -115.375) — documented Cu oxidation
SGM mineralogy: **chrysocolla (probable), azurite, malachite**, specularite, calcopirite, jarosite, quartz. That's a textbook **oxidized-Cu halo** — turquoise forms in exactly this assemblage. Prospect the oxidized zones around the old drift/catas; sample wall material.

### 🥉 ZONE C — La Turquesa core + El Hormiguero (30.05–30.09 N, 115.37–115.47 W)
The zone that named the belt. **El Hormiguero**: SGM notes "intense secondary alteration: **clays, Fe-oxides, calcite**" — the Al + oxidation half of the turquoise recipe, at scale ("masiva y altamente erosionada"). Look for blue-green on the eroded argillic slopes and in the gullies.

### 4th — ZONE D — **T2 Cerro la Turquesa (30.0485, -115.2362) — best INSIDE YOUR AREA**
* **Turquoise toponym at the point** (surface evidence of historic Cu)
* **Pale argillic mass** (~1 km W, our S2 survey) = the Al/clay source
* **NE–SW lineament** ~1 km E (B/R 0.60–0.64) = potential fault/fluid pathway
* Same latitude band as **La Bonita (30.048 N)** — the easternmost documented working
* **How to prospect it:** walk the gullies cutting the pale mass (fresh walls); 100 m rock-chip grid on the mass; pan + sample the drainage that leaves the mass; if the NE–SW band is a fault scarp, sample the scarp line for clay/Cu staining. Assay: **Cu (turquoise proxy), Au, Ag, Pb, Zn, Fe**.

### Secondary targets
* **La Bonita (30.048, -115.412)** — mineralized brecha N53°E/69°NW (0.7 m); brecha = permeable = fluid path
* **5 Minas + El Sacrificio (30.124, -115.344/-115.352)** — **tabular brechas** with Fe-Cu; SGM's explicit recommendation for future work: *"estructuras mineralizadas ciegas en forma sigmoidal y brechas tabulares"* (blind sigmoidal structures and tabular brechas)
* **Josefina (30.122, -115.360)** — Cu-Fe in **brecciated white granodiorite** (host-rock style reference)
* **The 30.03–30.05 N corridor** (La Bonita → T3 → T2): the E–W line tying the documented zone to your property — arroyo walls along it are the walking transect.

## Field protocol (one week, 2 people)

| Day | Where | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Zone A (El Cardonal–La Prieta–Esperanza) | Map works + fault; photo + sample all blue-green; wall chips along N82E fault |
| 2 | Zone B (San Martín) + Santa Martha | Oxidized-zone chips; look for turquoise in old drift walls |
| 3 | Zone C (La Turquesa core, El Hormiguero) | Argillic-slope survey; gully-wall sampling |
| 4 | Corridor La Bonita → T3 | Arroyo-wall transect; sediment pairs |
| 5 | Zone D (T2) + back to camp | Pale-mass grid; NE–SW band road-vs-fault check; samples |

Assay package per site: 10–20 rock chips + 2 sediment bags → Cu, Au, Ag, Pb, Zn, Fe (ICP-OES/MS); petrographic check on any blue-green/chrysocolla samples.

## Honest caveats

1. **El Cardonal is the only locality where SGM explicitly lists turquoise**; the rest are Cu-Fe workings with the *potential* for oxidized-Cu (turquoise) mineralization. Expect blue-green to be found, not assumed.
2. The **eastern half of the corridor (all three of your targets) is outside the mapped El Aguajito sheet** (sheet ends at 115°20′ W). The covering sheet (H11-B87 "Cerro el Huerfanito" and the eastern neighbor) is not in SGM's online catalog — **request from SGM (geoinfo@sgm.gob.mx)** to confirm lithology/alteration at your sites before spending.
3. Concession check before serious ground effort: minalia.sener.gob.mx lot map — the La Turquesa zone lots are documented in the SE/central sheet; verify coverage east to your property.
4. Water is the long-term gate (2023 Ley Minera), not the short-term one — prospecting is fine, but any development plan needs the CONAGUA baseline.
